A starting point

I’m not the leader, and this is not a set of rules that others should follow. Nor is it a pitch for leadership.

They are simply suggestions to give our community some opportunity for growth.

The idea, in brief

MapleForge is a community first. Companies can form from it, but the whole community does not need to become one company.

Personally, there should be no single leader while the group’s direction and focus are still taking shape. People also come looking for help, guidance and direction, so some practical structure would help.

STEP 1Know the group

Find out what active members are looking for.

STEP 2Support the space

Administrators help with permissions, channels and moderation.

STEP 3Let pods take shape

Create channels around real interest, with local governance only where needed.

Two areas to explore further: a support group and a place for projects, ideas and brainstorming.

Step 1

Know why people are here.

Make an introduction survey optional, but encouraged. There is already a Google Sheet; pass it along to people as they join and participate, rather than relying only on a pinned link.

Alongside the existing questions, ask a structured question: What are you looking for?

  • Networking: connections, job referrals and job sharing.
  • Employment advice: resumes, interviews and experiences with bad companies.
  • Building: products, projects, startups and brainstorming.
  • Starting a business: advice, incorporation, grants and RFPs.
  • Support: guidance, mental health and a support group.
  • Other: reform, protests or a place to express frustrations.

Use the answers to direct people and set priorities.

There are more than 300 members with different needs. Knowing who participates and what they want can help decide which channels deserve attention.

A hypothetical example: suppose 50 people respond out of 300. Focus on what those 50 need. If 90% want networking, support that channel with moderators, administrators and useful templates. Others may become active when they find something valuable.

These numbers illustrate the proposal. They are not survey results.

Step 2

Administrators help the community function.

A general administrator group would look after permissions, channels and moderation. Channel leadership is a separate role, although a person could contribute in both roles.

  • Keep spam, abuse and general violations out of the shared space.
  • Encourage members to answer the introduction questions.
  • Consider a bot that asks new members and explains why: to help the community grow and direct people to the right place.

The purpose: support a growing community without overly dictating what people need or what they should do.

Step 3

Build channels around actual needs.

The suggested categories are a starting guess. Check whether they fit and adjust them based on members’ answers and discussions.

Each primary channel or pod would work out the structure it needs:

  • A small group: five people can work differently from fifty. With fewer people, less governance may be needed. Let it develop.
  • A larger group: elect channel leaders where needed, preferably more than one.
  • Practical rules and templates: add them where useful for that pod. For example, a job-sharing format should include a link; spam or scam links could carry a ban.

Leadership and governance would develop within the relevant pod, according to its needs and size.

A possible pod / Support

Help people through a layoff.

Members already share support hotlines, support groups, city services, food banks and other resources. A support pod could build on that.

Some members have experience in HR, support work, guidance counselling or related roles. They could help others while they look for work themselves. Real community service could also become experience on their resume.

An example for your resume

Support Worker, MapleForge Community, 2026

Community service helping people who have experienced recent layoffs, providing resources and guidance.

An illustrative role in the proposal, not an existing appointment.

A possible pod / Projects, ideas & brainstorming

Let teams form. Keep the connections useful.

  1. Keep a general brainstorming channel. The community already has one.
  2. Let projects organize themselves. When a project takes off, its team can ask the administrators for a channel.
  3. Offer useful connections. Help teams find members with the skills and roles they need. Provide a place to showcase products to the community. If a project succeeds, encourage it to remember and give back to the community where it began.
  4. Keep a consistent project directory. A simple template could make an active list of MapleForge projects easier to browse, while leaving teams free to work their own way.
  5. Let each team choose its members. A project might welcome everyone or seek specific skills and roles. Even an administrator should not be able to force their way into a project.

Teams may eventually move away from the server. Shared services and connections can still give them a reason to stay involved.

A personal view

Ideas need execution, and sales.

Personally, I would put worries about stolen ideas and NDAs aside for now, and concentrate on getting something built.

  • Many people have good ideas. Can they execute them?
  • If someone can do it alone, they should go for it.
  • If a team can build it, can it sell it? Identify members with sales experience or networks they can reach out to.
  • Even with AI, there is a long road beyond the idea. Focus first on getting the work done.
